# Project Isekai (pre-alpha)

A cosy low-poly 3D action-life RPG for Windows. You're summoned into a round fantasy city that runs on the real clock and seasons. Fight and explore, rise through the Adventurer's Guild, fish and catch bugs, farm and cook, decorate a house and garden, dress up, and make friends with the townsfolk. It comes with **Isekai Studio**, for making your own content without code.

> **Pre-alpha:** expect bugs and things that change between builds. Saves from one pre-alpha may not load in the next.

## Download

Get the latest zip from **[Releases](../../releases)**, unzip it anywhere, and run `Project Isekai/Project Isekai.exe`. From Pre-alpha 3.2 on, the game tells you on the title screen when a new version is out and can update itself (**Update now**); Settings → Online turns the check off. The `README.md` and `docs/how-to-play.md` inside the zip explain the controls and your first day.

**Requirements:** Windows 10 or 11 (64-bit), and a graphics card that supports Vulkan or DirectX 12. You need about 800 MB of disk space. Keyboard and mouse or a gamepad both work.

## What's in this build

- **The first hour (new in 6.3):** you arrive with nothing on screen, and the game opens one piece at a time as you meet the people who own it: Mabel gives you her old satchel, Tobin a bale and his map, Elena signs you up with one choice and hands you a licence card, Bram talks the moment you step into his forge, and Hal, the guild's drill-master, teaches your first skill at the training ground. A tracked questline, "Getting Settled into a New World", points the way, and a letter nobody should have been able to send waits at the Post Office. New weapons start with their basic attack; Hal teaches the first four skills as your proficiency grows (existing characters keep every skill they had).
- **The world:** a city, plains, forest, coast, an island village, Duskhollow (a hamlet under the forest canopy that keeps night hours) and four dungeons, with real-time seasons, weather, tides and moon phases; clouds cast drifting shadows and sunbeams slant through the gaps.
- **The look (6.0 and 6.1):** everything in our own art: trees with season sets, ground cover, flowers and crops, the town's buildings (lit fanlights over the doors at night), the town's and the countryside's props, the twelve bodies on a new rig with hair that reads from every side, and every creature, from the bunny to Grandmother Clatterclaw in her house; wind in the foliage, the windmill and the banners; ducks, herons, butterflies, bats and an owl about by season and hour; our own music and sounds throughout; Colour vision and Warning patterns for the boss warnings, comfort settings (hold-or-toggle, captions with a direction arrow, high contrast), and a graphics preset picked for your card on first launch.
- **The dungeons:** the Mossy Hollow (the King's Court and the Roots below it), the Glimmer Grotto, the Old Dig's catacombs and the Old Silver Mine, all on one standard: dark rooms lit by lamps, moss and crystals, levers and timed gates, traps, checkpoints, chests, a map, and a boss at the end. New in 6.3: every dungeon carries a guild rank (the four are D-rank), shown at the door, on the map and on quest cards; a lower-ranked adventurer is warned once on the way in, and the door stays open. At rank D Elena hands you the guild's D-rank list, and the board offers dungeon cards: clear the boss, light every lamp in one run, a clean run with no falls, chests and secrets, and two rescues (Juniper asleep in the Hollow, Ambrose down to the wick in the Old Dig). The Slime King's Waltz is a three-act dance: bounces to a beat, bits that charge down red lines and carom off each other, and a last slide you end in a corner. Grandmother Clatterclaw fights in a nest that floods the moment you step in: rocks rolling behind a red band, a tide that pushes and pulls with whirlpools either side of her, claw hooks that drag you in, crablings lobbed at you three at a time, and an old sluice whose surge knocks her flat.
- **Combat:** real-time combat with dodging, statuses, weapon mastery, clear swing arcs, an aim marker for bows and glowing arrow trails, and the club, which turns a charge straight back. Your skills are a **constellation**: nine trees drawn as a star chart, each skill earning its own experience and growing along its branch.
- **The realms:** settlements grow through stages as you spend and help; invest in shares, weather the farms' troubles, meet the seasonal traders and the Merchant Hero, and come back to greetings that tell you the news since you left.
- **The bestiary:** 58 creatures, each with magic, rare and unique versions that look different (bigger, horned, maned, armoured) and fight differently. A unique turns up at most once a day.
- **Life:** fishing, bug catching (new in 6.2: fourteen bugs with their own models that flap, crawl, hop and glow, and the net catches the one you aim at), farming, cooking, pets, a museum, jobs, festivals and mail. Talk to people to hand in quests and open their shops; weather boards round town show the week ahead.
- **Your gear pack:** extra slots for weapons and armour and a pocket for 99 of every monster drop, upgradeable. Quest items and keepsakes live in their own Key items tab and take no bag slots.
- **The guild:** a real quest board in the guild hall (new in 6.3: walk up and press E to take quests at any hour; every card leads with its kind and an icon, with a Lessons row and the next licence's row greyed), three new quest kinds (deliver a piece of gear by rarity, donate to the museum, say hello to people), a board that rewards variety (a quest nobody has taken in a while pays a premium), and a post office in the city for anyone's post.
- **Your home:** a house and yard to decorate and paint, a workbench with plans, cabins, and a photo album.
- **Your look:** outfits for every slot, dyes, and a sewing lesson.
- **Symbol art:** make pictures from layered shapes (with a gamepad or a mouse) and share them in chat, on the bulletin board, in letters and in a frame on your wall.
- **Online:** friends, the Crossroads Room, letters to far-away friends, leaderboards, and co-op that works even behind home routers.
- **Little things to find:** returning rare creatures, mystery threads, mini-games (skating, kites, darts), footprints in the snow, and the Milky Way on dark nights.
- **Isekai Studio:** the content editor the game itself is built with, now with tabs, side-by-side pages, pop-out windows, an ability builder and creature tiers.

## Playing online

Online play is on. The game connects to the official server by itself: open **Esc → Friends & parties** to get your friend code, add friends by theirs, and meet in the Crossroads Room.

- **Settings → Online** shows whether you're connected, and has a **Test connection** button.
- If you can't connect, the game keeps working offline and tries again later.
- You can point the game at a different server in Settings → Online; **Official server** puts it back.

## Known limits

- **Saves made in 6.3 won't open in an older build** (the save format changed for the first hour). Updating keeps your saves and settings as always.
- **The Crossroads Room** looks for company only in your own region until the server's 6.1 update goes out; the game works either way.
- **The official server is new.** Expect the odd restart while it settles in.

## Reporting bugs

Please open an **[issue](../../issues)** saying what you were doing and what happened. Attach your error log if you can: in the game, Settings → **Open the logs folder**, then `errors.log`.
